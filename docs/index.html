Overview

When "normal" is not a template

Existing point cloud anomaly detection benchmarks are designed for industrial parts, where normal samples share standard geometric shapes. Agricultural products grow naturally: their shape, size and texture vary widely, so "normal" is far less well-defined, and industrial benchmarks and methods do not transfer well.

We build NaturalGrowth, the first point cloud anomaly detection benchmark for agricultural products, and propose GESA, which injects geometric priors at two levels. ASPE fuses local curvature and density into the position embedding to enhance the perception of anomalous regions, and GAA adds geometric biases and a spectral branch to attention to strengthen local structure modeling. Experiments on NaturalGrowth, Real3D-AD and Anomaly-ShapeNet show competitive performance with a single unified model per dataset.

Benchmark

NaturalGrowth

31 categories of common agricultural products, scanned with an industrial structured-light scanner on a turntable for full 360° coverage. Defects are real: natural ones such as wormholes and rot, and physical damage from picking and transport. Every defective sample has point-level ground truth.

Method

GESA: geometry priors in embedding and attention

ASPE

Anomaly-Sensitive Position Embedding. Local curvature and density modulate a Fourier position embedding, so potentially anomalous regions get a stronger positional signal.

GAA

Geometry-Aware Attention. Distance and normal-similarity biases are added to the attention scores, making attention aware of 3D structure.

Spectral branch

An FFT-domain branch is fused with spatial attention. Global frequency patterns stay robust to the irregular shapes of natural products.
